Title: First aid for cuts and bleeding
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/cuts-and-grazes

To stop bleeding, press firmly on the wound with a clean cloth or bandage. Keep pressing without lifting the cloth to check. If the injury is on an arm or leg, raise it above the level of the heart if possible, and have the person sit or lie down.

For a small cut or graze, wash your hands, rinse the wound under clean running water, pat the area dry and cover it with a clean dressing. Change the dressing if it gets wet or dirty.

Get urgent medical help or call 108 or 112 if the bleeding is heavy, spurts out, or does not stop after 10 minutes of firm pressure. Do not remove an object that is stuck in a wound; press around it and get help.

See a doctor for a cut that is deep, wide or gaping, is very dirty, was caused by an animal or human bite, or is on the face. Also see a doctor if the wound becomes red, swollen, warm, painful or leaks pus, or if you have fever. Ask a doctor whether you need a tetanus shot after a dirty or deep wound.
