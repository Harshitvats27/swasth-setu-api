Title: Fever in adults
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/fever-in-adults

A fever means your body temperature is higher than normal, usually around 38°C or above. It is often a sign that the body is fighting an infection such as a cold or flu. Many fevers get better on their own within a few days.

Things that can help at home:
- Rest as much as you can.
- Drink plenty of fluids, ideally water, so that your urine stays light yellow and clear.
- Wear light clothing and keep the room comfortably cool.
- Stay at home and avoid mixing with other people while you have a fever.

See a doctor soon if the fever does not improve after a few days, keeps coming back, or gets worse despite home care. Get medical help the same day if you also have a stiff neck, a rash that does not fade, trouble breathing, chest pain, confusion, repeated vomiting, a seizure, or if you cannot keep fluids down.

Older people, pregnant women and people with long-term illnesses such as diabetes, heart disease or kidney disease should see a doctor sooner. Ask a doctor or pharmacist before taking any medicine.
