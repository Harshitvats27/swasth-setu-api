Title: Suspected broken bone or sprain
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/broken-bone

A broken bone (fracture) may cause severe pain, swelling, bruising, a bent or odd shape, or being unable to move or put weight on the injured part. A sprain is an injury to the ligaments around a joint and can look similar.

If you think a bone may be broken:
- Keep the injured part as still as possible. Do not try to straighten it or push a bone back in.
- Support the area with a sling, a folded cloth or by resting it on a cushion.
- Wrap an ice pack or cold pack in a cloth and hold it on the area to reduce swelling. Do not put ice directly on the skin.
- Do not eat or drink anything in case an operation is needed.
- Go to a hospital emergency department.

Call 108 or 112 if the injury is from a serious fall or accident, if a bone is sticking out through the skin, if there is heavy bleeding, if the neck or back may be injured, or if the person is pale, faint or very unwell. Do not move a person with a suspected neck or back injury unless they are in danger.
