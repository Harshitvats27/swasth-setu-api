Title: Diabetes basics
Source: https://www.nhs.uk/conditions/diabetes

Diabetes is a lifelong condition in which there is too much sugar (glucose) in the blood. The body either does not make enough insulin or cannot use it properly. The main types are type 1, type 2 and gestational diabetes, which can appear during pregnancy.

Common symptoms are feeling very thirsty, passing urine more often, feeling tired, losing weight without trying, blurred vision, cuts or wounds that heal slowly, and frequent infections. Some people with type 2 diabetes have no symptoms for years, so testing is important if you are at risk.

Only a doctor can diagnose diabetes, using blood tests. Anyone with these symptoms, or with a family history of diabetes, should see a doctor for a check.

Healthy habits that help prevent type 2 diabetes or keep it under control:
- Eat a balanced diet with less sugar, sweets and fried food.
- Stay active and keep a healthy weight.
- Avoid tobacco.
- Go for regular check-ups, including eye, foot and kidney checks if you have diabetes.

Get urgent medical help if a person with diabetes becomes very drowsy or confused, vomits repeatedly, breathes deeply and fast, or has breath that smells fruity.
